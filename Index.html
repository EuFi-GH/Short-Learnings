What is Microsoft Copilot?

Think of Copilot as a smart assistant that lives right inside your Microsoft 365 apps. It reads, writes, summarises, and analyses — so you can do more in less time.

Microsoft 365
Powered by your existing apps
AI-Powered
Built on large language models
The simple explanation

Microsoft Copilot is an artificial intelligence (AI) assistant built into your Microsoft 365 tools. It can understand plain English questions and instructions — called prompts — and respond with helpful text, summaries, plans, or data.

Copilot is powered by large language models (LLMs) — the same type of technology behind popular AI tools — but it's deeply integrated with your work data in Microsoft 365. This means it can reference your emails, documents, meetings, and calendars (with your permission) to give you more relevant results.

Where does Copilot live?
Copilot.microsoft.com

The web-based Copilot chat interface — a bit like a chat window where you can ask anything, draft content, or research topics. Great for general tasks and quick questions.

Inside your Microsoft 365 Apps

Copilot is embedded directly into Word, Outlook, Teams, Excel, and PowerPoint. Click the Copilot button in the ribbon or sidebar to access it right where you're working.

How does it actually work?
1

You type a prompt

You tell Copilot what you need in plain English — for example: "Draft a welcome email for a new student starting their Certificate III next Monday."

2

Copilot processes your request

Copilot uses AI to understand your instruction. If you're in Outlook, it may reference your past emails and contacts to make the response more relevant.

3

You review and refine

Copilot provides a response — a draft email, a summary, a list, or an answer. You always review it and make adjustments before using it. You're always in control.

4

You use the result

Send the email, paste the content into a document, or use the summary in your next team meeting. Copilot saves you time — it doesn't replace your judgment.

What Copilot is great at
✍️

Drafting & writing

Emails, letters, reports, policies, proposals, and social media content.

📋

Summarising

Long email threads, meeting notes, documents, and reports — distilled into key points.

🔍

Researching & answering

Background information, explanations, and answers to questions — instantly.

📊

Analysing data

In Excel, Copilot can interpret your data, suggest charts, and explain trends.

💬

Rewriting & editing

Make text more professional, shorter, friendlier, or in plain English.

📅

Planning & organising

Create agendas, action lists, project plans, and timelines from dot points.

⚠️ Important — Always Review

Copilot is very capable, but it can sometimes get things wrong. Always read through what it produces before using, sending, or publishing anything. Use your own knowledge and judgment — especially for compliance-related content at our RTO.

  • Type: "What are five ways AI can help someone working in student enrolments?"
  • Notice how the response looks — can you ask a follow-up question to get more detail?

Copilot for Student Services

From enrolment emails to compliance letters — Copilot can handle the writing and formatting, so you can focus on the students.

Your everyday student services tasks — made faster

Student-facing staff often handle high volumes of similar communications — enrolment confirmations, orientation information, progress updates, RPL requests, and withdrawal letters. Copilot is excellent at producing consistent, professional communications quickly.

Real scenarios from your role
👩‍💼
Sarah — Enrolments Officer
Student Services Team
Task: Sarah needs to send an enrolment confirmation email to a new student starting their Diploma of Community Services. It needs to include start date, orientation details, and what to bring on Day 1.

Draft a professional and friendly enrolment confirmation email for a new student starting the Diploma of Community Services on 3 February 2025. Include: orientation is 8:30am on the same day, they need to bring their USI number and photo ID, and they can contact us at enrolments@rto.edu.au with any questions. Use Australian English.
✅ Copilot generates a complete, ready-to-review email in seconds. Sarah reads it, adjusts the student's name, and sends. Time saved: ~15 minutes per email.
👨‍💼
James — Student Registration Officer
Admin & Registration
Task: James receives 30+ RPL enquiries each month. He needs to write clear, empathetic responses explaining the RPL process — but each one takes him 20 minutes.
📝  James's Copilot Prompt
Write a clear and supportive email response explaining our RPL (Recognition of Prior Learning) process to a prospective student who has asked how it works. Keep it plain English, warm in tone, and include that they'll need to provide a portfolio of evidence and that we offer a free RPL consultation. Use Australian English.
✅ James has a template response in under a minute. He reviews and personalises it slightly for each enquiry. Time saved: 15+ minutes per response.

Summarise this withdrawal policy document in plain English for students. Keep it to one page, use clear headings, and highlight the key steps a student needs to take if they want to withdraw from a course. Make it easy to read for someone with limited English literacy.

More ways Copilot helps Student Services
📬 Email Management

Use Copilot in Outlook to summarise long email chains, draft responses to student enquiries, and create standard reply templates for common questions like "When does my course start?" or "How do I defer?"

📄 Document Creation

Generate course handouts, orientation schedules, FAQ sheets, and information guides in Word. Copilot can format these professionally with headings and dot points.

📊 Reporting

Use Copilot in Excel to analyse enrolment data, summarise student completion rates, and identify patterns — then explain what the numbers mean in plain language.

🗓️ Scheduling

Ask Copilot in Teams or Outlook to help draft orientation schedules, class timetable communications, or reminders for upcoming assessment due dates.

Practice Activity
Write an enrolment email

Open Copilot and try this yourself. Customise the prompt with your own RTO's real details:

  1. Go to Copilot (copilot.microsoft.com or open Outlook → Copilot sidebar)
  2. Type: "Draft a friendly enrolment confirmation email for a student starting [your course name] on [date]. Include the time and location, what to bring, and who to contact."

  4. Ask it to shorten it: "Can you shorten this to under 150 words?"

Notice how you can keep refining by asking follow-up questions — you don't need to start from scratch each time.

Finding Copilot in your apps
Where to look
  • 📌
    Word, Excel, PowerPoint: Look for the Copilot button in the Home ribbon at the top, or right-click selected text to see Copilot options.
  • 📌
    Outlook: Open an email, click Copilot in the ribbon to summarise or draft a reply. When composing, look for "Draft with Copilot."
  • 📌
    Teams: During a meeting with transcription on, click the Copilot icon in the meeting toolbar. In chat, type /Copilot to start.
  • 📌
    Microsoft 365 (web): Visit copilot.microsoft.com and sign in with your work account for the general Copilot chat experience.

For Copilot to summarise your Teams meetings, transcription must be turned on at the start of the meeting. Ask your IT team to enable this by default, or turn it on yourself at the start of each meeting: More options → Start transcription.

Writing Great Prompts

The quality of what Copilot gives you depends almost entirely on what you ask. Here's how to get the best results every time — no technical knowledge required.

What is a prompt?

A prompt is simply the instruction or question you type into Copilot. Think of it like briefing a very capable colleague — the clearer and more detailed your brief, the better the result. Vague instructions get vague results. Specific instructions get great ones.

The CLEAR Prompt Framework

Use this five-part framework to build prompts that get consistently good results:

C

Context — Set the scene

Tell Copilot who you are, what your organisation does, and any relevant background. Example: "I work in student enrolments at a registered training organisation in Queensland..."

L

Length & Format — Say how long and how

Specify whether you want a bullet list, a formal letter, a short paragraph, or a full document. Example: "...in a friendly email under 150 words..."

E

Examples — Show what good looks like

If you have a previous email or document you like, paste it in and say "write something in this style." Examples help enormously. Example: "...similar in tone to the example below..."

A

Audience — Who is this for?

Always tell Copilot who will read the output. A student email needs different language than an employer proposal. Example: "...for a student who may have limited English literacy..."

R

Role — Tell Copilot who to be

Ask Copilot to take on a persona if it helps. Example: "Act as a friendly RTO student support officer writing to a student who needs encouragement to complete their course."

Good vs. not-so-good prompts
❌ Vague Prompt
Write me an email about a course.

No context, no audience, no purpose, no tone guidance. Copilot will produce something generic and probably not useful.

✅ Specific Prompt
Draft a friendly reminder email to a student who has not submitted their Assessment 2 for the Certificate IV in Community Services. The due date was last Friday. Remind them of the late submission process, offer support, and include our student support line: 1800 XXX XXX. Keep it warm and non-threatening. Under 120 words. Australian English.

Clear purpose, audience, tone, content requirements, and word limit. Copilot will produce something you can actually use.

Prompting tips for common RTO tasks
For emails

Always include: who you're writing to, what the email is about, the desired outcome, and word length. Add "Australian English" and "professional but warm tone" as defaults.

For documents

Tell Copilot the document type, who will read it, and what sections to include. For policy documents, add "use plain English and clear headings."

For marketing content

Describe the target audience in detail. Include platform (LinkedIn, Facebook, email), desired action, and whether you want multiple options to choose from.

For summaries

Tell Copilot what to focus on. "Summarise this and highlight the action items" gives a better result than "summarise this." Specify length (3 bullet points, half a page, etc.).

💡 Keep refining — it's a conversation

You don't need to write the perfect prompt first time. Start with your best attempt, then refine by asking follow-up questions. "Can you make this shorter?", "Change the tone to be more formal", "Add a section about payment plans" — Copilot remembers the conversation context and keeps improving.

Practice Activity
Refine your prompts

Pick a task you do regularly and try building a great prompt using the CLEAR framework:

  1. Write your first attempt at a prompt for a real task you do.
  2. Now look at it against CLEAR — have you included Context, Length/Format, Examples (if relevant), Audience, and Role?
  3. Add any missing elements and try the improved version in Copilot.
  4. Compare the results — how much better is the output with the detailed prompt?

Share your best prompt with a colleague — building a team library of good prompts is one of the fastest ways to get value from Copilot.

Using Copilot Responsibly

Copilot is powerful — and with that comes responsibility. Here's how to protect student data, use AI ethically, and stay on the right side of our compliance obligations.

Why responsible use matters for our RTO

As an RTO, we handle sensitive student information — names, dates of birth, USI numbers, health information, financial details, and assessment records. We have obligations under the Privacy Act 1988, ASQA standards, and our own data governance policies. Using Copilot responsibly means protecting this information and using AI output with appropriate judgment.

What to share — and what not to

✓ Safe to use with Copilot

  • Generic course information and descriptions
  • Draft text you're writing (without personal student details)
  • Industry or general research questions
  • Anonymised data or summary statistics
  • Policy documents and public information
  • Your own meeting notes and work content

✕ Do NOT enter into Copilot

  • Student names, USI numbers, or dates of birth
  • Student financial or payment details
  • Medical or disability information about individuals
  • Passwords, login credentials, or system access
  • Commercially sensitive pricing or contracts
  • Another person's private communications without permission
⚠️ Important Note on Microsoft Copilot & Data

When you use Microsoft 365 Copilot through your work account, your data is processed under Microsoft's enterprise data protection terms. This is different from free consumer AI tools. However, our organisation's policies still apply — if you're unsure whether something is appropriate to use, check with your manager or the IT team first.

Reviewing AI output — your responsibility
🔍 Always fact-check

Copilot can confidently produce content that is incorrect. For anything compliance-related, training package information, or factual claims — verify against the source. Don't assume Copilot is right.

✏️ Always personalise

AI-generated content can sound generic or impersonal. Before sending any student communication, read it as a human and make sure it sounds like your RTO — warm, professional, and genuine.

📋 Keep your judgment

Copilot is a tool, not a decision-maker. For complex student matters (complaints, welfare concerns, RPL decisions), use Copilot for drafting assistance only — the decision must be yours.

📁 Document appropriately

If you use Copilot to draft a formal student communication or document, ensure the final version is reviewed by the appropriate person before it's sent or filed in the student management system.

Ethical use of AI at our RTO
  • ✓
    Use Copilot to assist your work, not replace human judgment on important matters
  • ✓
    Be transparent — if you've used AI to draft a document that others will rely on, note that it was AI-assisted and reviewed by you
  • ✓
    Do not use Copilot to produce content that could be misleading, discriminatory, or harmful to students
  • ✓
    If Copilot produces content you're uncomfortable with, don't use it — report it to your manager
  • ✓
    Follow our RTO's AI Use Policy (available on the intranet) for any specific guidance relevant to your role
💡 When in doubt — ask before you post

If you're unsure whether it's appropriate to use Copilot for a particular task or whether output is accurate enough to use — ask your manager. It's always better to check than to send something that causes a compliance issue or damages student trust.

Reflection Activity
Spot the risks

Review each of these scenarios and decide: Is this an appropriate use of Copilot? What (if anything) needs to change?

  1. A registration officer asks Copilot: "Draft an email to John Smith (DOB 15/03/1990, USI: 3XKYL9F) confirming his enrolment..."
  2. A marketing officer asks: "Write me a LinkedIn post about our new Hospitality course starting next term."

  4. A trainer asks Copilot to summarise an external assessment tool document to help them design a unit of learning.

Discuss your answers with a colleague. There are no trick questions — the goal is to build good habits around what to use Copilot for and how to review the output.
